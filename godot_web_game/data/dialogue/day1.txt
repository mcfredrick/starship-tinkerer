# Day one: arriving, Odessa's first commission, the club race invitation.

== intro
* The hangar doors grind open on Quietwater Dock. Dust motes drift through a slab of Bramble-light.
* Workbenches. A tea nook with a dented kettle. Drawers labeled things like "BOLTS (MOSTLY)" and "DO NOT."
* And in the back bay, under a tarp, something round and stubborn-looking.
* You set your bag down on the nearest workbench. Everything you own fits in it. On top is...
> A coolant spanner, worn smooth from a thousand night shifts. => freighter
> A dog-eared engineering textbook, full of your own notes. => academy
> A lucky bolt you pulled from your very first scrapped engine. => salvage
-- freighter
* You turn the spanner over. The Unquiet Margin's crest is still stamped on the handle. You never did give it back.
! set backstory_freighter
=> tablet
-- academy
* The textbook falls open where it always does: a page on drive harmonics, with "but WHY does it hum?" scrawled in the margin in your hand.
! set backstory_academy
=> tablet
-- salvage
* You put the bolt in your pocket, where it belongs. Nan Tallow said a tinkerer should always carry the first thing they ever saved.
! set backstory_salvage
-- tablet
* Your tablet chimes. There's a letter from Halloran, and the dock bulletin.
end

== odessa_arrives
* The shop bell jangles. A small grey hauler noses into the bay, rattling like a drawer full of spoons.
odessa happy: Hello, hello! Oh, you must be {name}. Halloran said you'd be here.
odessa: I'm Odessa. Odessa Vell. And this is the Pocket Moth. Say hello, Moth.
* The Moth's exhaust coughs a sad little smoke ring.
odessa thoughtful: She's not as young as she was. Neither am I, mind you, but at least I don't rattle.
> What can I do for you? => ask
> Halloran told me about you. => halloran
-- halloran
odessa laughing: Did they! All good things, I hope. Or at least funny things.
odessa: Halloran fixed this old girl more times than I can count. I suppose it's your turn now.
=> ask
-- ask
odessa: It's my grandkids. Three of them, scattered across three moons. I'm taking them to see the comet bloom next week.
odessa sad: But the cabin rattles so loud they can't hear a word I say. Last trip my grandson told me a whole story about a comet and I just nodded along.
odessa sad: I nodded at a story. About a comet. To a seven-year-old.
odessa: Could you make her quieter? Comfier wouldn't hurt either. I've got two hundred and twenty pebs, and I'll throw in a seed cake or two.
> Leave it with me. => accept
> Two hundred and twenty is a bit tight... => haggle
-- haggle
odessa thoughtful: Is it? Well. It's what I've got, love. But the cakes are very good. Ask anyone on the dock.
> All right, deal. => accept
-- accept
odessa happy: Wonderful! I'll go get a cocoa at the drum gardens and come back this afternoon.
odessa: Oh, and {name}? She's got a few quirks. Give her a good scan first. Halloran always did.
! job odessa_1
end

== odessa_1_delighted
* Odessa walks a slow circle around the Moth, one hand trailing along the hull.
odessa surprised: Is that... is she running? I can barely hear her!
odessa laughing: Listen to that! It's like a cat thinking about a nap!
odessa happy: Oh, {name}. The grandkids are going to talk my ears off and I'm going to hear every single word.
odessa happy: Here. Your pebs, a little extra for the trouble, and two seed cakes. Don't argue. You're too skinny for a tinkerer.
end

== odessa_1_pleased
odessa happy: Well now! That's a real improvement. She sounds like a ship that's had a good night's sleep.
odessa: Here's your pebs, and the seed cakes as promised. Thank you, {name}.
end

== odessa_1_lukewarm
odessa thoughtful: Hmm. She's... a little better, I think? It's hard to tell over the noise.
odessa: Well. I'll make do. I've made do for sixty years, I can make do for a comet.
odessa: Here's what we agreed, and the seed cakes. Maybe next time, eh?
end

== racers_visit
* The bay lights flicker as a long scarlet racer glides past the doors and settles with a click.
ilse grumpy: So you're the new tinkerer. Halloran's pick.
ilse thoughtful: Hm. Your hands look like they know what they're doing. That's something.
* A boxy yellow tug clanks in behind her. A small robot pops its head out of the hatch.
brindle happy: Hello! I am Brindle. I am here to observe you. In a friendly way.
ilse: Brindle follows me around. It times everything.
brindle surprised: Not everything. Only things that happen. You said hello in 1.4 seconds. That is a very good hello.
ilse: Club race tonight. Three laps round the ring. You've got that wreck in the back, haven't you? Halloran's Kettle.
> She's not a wreck. She's a project. => proud
> She's... very much a wreck. => honest
-- proud
ilse laughing: Ha! Halloran said exactly that. Every week, for eleven years.
=> invite
-- honest
brindle happy: I like her. She is round. Round things are friendly.
=> invite
-- invite
ilse: Bring her out tonight if you're brave. Prize pebs for the podium. Bring her out next week if you're sensible.
brindle: I will fit my bubble emitter. It goes pop. Pop is my favorite sound.
ilse grumpy: Don't tell the competition about your gadgets, Brindle.
brindle thoughtful: Oh. Is {name} the competition? I thought {name} was a friend.
ilse happy: Both, if we're lucky.
! set race_open
end

== race_won
ilse surprised: The Kettle! Beat the Heron! In a straight race!
ilse laughing: Halloran is going to be insufferable when they hear about this.
brindle laughing: New lap record for a teakettle! It is also the first lap record for a teakettle. Both records are yours!
end

== race_podium
ilse happy: Not bad for a teakettle. Not bad at all.
brindle happy: You went zoom. I went zoom. We all went zoom!
end

== race_back
ilse thoughtful: Well, you finished. Most first-timers spin out on the sun-hairpin and cry.
brindle happy: Your Kettle has a very nice whistle when it is being passed.
ilse: Tune her up. Come back next week.
end


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- your past

== rook_visit
* A long, battered freighter eases past the bay doors, too big to come inside. A familiar voice crackles over the dock radio.
rook happy: There's my night-shift engineer! Come out here, {name}, I'm not squeezing the Margin through that door.
* Captain Mabe Rook stands on the gantry, cap pushed back, exactly as you left her.
rook thoughtful: They're scrapping the Margin at the end of the season. Thirty years she's carried me. I wanted her running sweet one last time, and there's only one engineer I trust with her coolant.
rook: And I wanted to see what you'd made of yourself. Halloran wrote to me, you know. Said you'd do.
> I left without saying goodbye properly. I'm sorry. => sorry
> It's good to see you, Captain. => good
-- sorry
rook happy: You left to do this. That's the best goodbye there is.
=> job
-- good
rook laughing: Captain! Still! Ha. You always did call me Captain when you'd broken something.
-- job
rook: Four hundred pebs, and she wants to be quiet and smooth for her last runs. Like you used to keep her.
! set past_visit
! job rook_1
end

== rook_1_delighted
rook surprised: Listen to her. She hasn't sounded like that since you were aboard.
rook happy: She'll finish her life singing. Thank you, {name}. Here, and the crest plate off the bridge door. She'd want you to have it.
end

== rook_1_pleased
rook happy: She's running sweeter. That'll do her proud. Thank you.
end

== rook_1_lukewarm
rook thoughtful: Still rattles a bit. Well, so do I. Here, and thank you for trying.
end

== ashgrove_visit
* A neat little saucer lands precisely in the center of the bay. A familiar figure steps out, smoothing their robe.
ashgrove thoughtful: {name}. I've come to see whether you were right.
ashgrove: You left Ringward one term short of your degree. You told me you'd rather fix old ships than design new ones. I told you you were wasting yourself.
ashgrove happy: Halloran wrote to me, after. Sent me a sketch of the Kettle's drive you'd rebuilt. I've been thinking about it for weeks.
> I wasn't wasting myself. I was learning by ear. => ear
> I'm sorry I disappointed you. => sorry
-- ear
ashgrove surprised: By ear. Yes. That's what Halloran said too.
=> job
-- sorry
ashgrove sad: You didn't. I was afraid you'd prove me wrong, and you have, and I find I'm glad of it.
-- job
ashgrove: The Theorem is my own ship. She's perfect on paper and miserable to fly. Show me what you'd do with her.
! set past_visit
! job ashgrove_1
end

== ashgrove_1_delighted
ashgrove surprised: That's not in any textbook. I'd like you to come and lecture on it. Once. Wearing whatever you like.
ashgrove happy: Here. And your degree, if you want it. The board agreed a practical can count. I insisted.
end

== ashgrove_1_pleased
ashgrove happy: Better. Unorthodox, but better. I'll be writing this up.
end

== ashgrove_1_lukewarm
ashgrove thoughtful: Interesting. Not what I'd have done. Not entirely wrong. Here.
end

== tallow_visit
* A patched-up salvage tug clatters into the bay, trailing a smell of oil and moon-dust. Out climbs a small green figure in a red headscarf, beaming.
tallow laughing: There's my little scrap-rat! Look at you, with your own bay! Halloran said you'd grown, but look at you!
tallow happy: Got my fare saved up and the old Magpie running, just about, and I thought, I'll go and see where that bolt of mine ended up.
> I've still got the bolt, Nan. => bolt
> I've missed you. => missed
-- bolt
tallow surprised: You never! Show me. Oh. Oh, you have.
=> job
-- missed
tallow happy: Course you have. Everybody misses me. I'm very missable.
-- job
tallow thoughtful: Now. The Magpie's running, but only just, and I'd like to get home again in her. Mates' rates? Three hundred pebs and a jar of moon-dust jam.
! set past_visit
! job tallow_1
end

== tallow_1_delighted
tallow laughing: She purrs! The Magpie's never purred in her life! I raised you right, I did.
tallow happy: Here. And the yard's old sign, the one you painted when you were seven. It's got your handprints on it.
end

== tallow_1_pleased
tallow happy: She'll get me home and then some. Good work, scrap-rat.
end

== tallow_1_lukewarm
tallow thoughtful: She'll get me home. Probably. I'll take the long way, near the yards.
end

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Halloran comes home

== halloran_home
* The shop bell jangles. Sprocket, asleep on the counter, opens one eye, then both, then is off the counter and across the bay in a streak of black and white.
* A weathered figure in a long coat is kneeling by the door, and Sprocket is headbutting their hand as if to knock it off.
halloran laughing: Hello, you old menace. Yes. Yes, I know. I'm sorry. I'm here.
halloran happy: And you must be {name}. Look at this place. You've kept it. You've done more than keep it.
? backstory_freighter => freighter
? backstory_academy => academy
halloran thoughtful: I watched you rebuild a dead drive in Nan Tallow's yard once, with nothing but a lucky bolt and spite. I knew then. I'm glad I was right.
=> choice
-- freighter
halloran thoughtful: I fixed the Margin's coolant loop once, years ago, and found a night-shift engineer had already fixed it better, in the dark, by ear. I knew then. I'm glad I was right.
=> choice
-- academy
halloran thoughtful: Ondine Ashgrove sent me your notes. Page after page of "but why does it hum?" Nobody asks that any more. I knew then. I'm glad I was right.
-- choice
halloran: I've heard the ships again, out past the comet line. I'm not here to take the shop back. It's yours, if you'll have it. But I'd like to know what you want it to be.
> It's still Halloran's. I'll keep it for you. => keep
> Stay. We'll run it together. => partners
> Paint a new sign. It's mine now, and you're always welcome. => mine
-- keep
halloran happy: That's kind. Then I'll come back every spring and make a nuisance of myself, and the sign stays crooked.
! set shop_keep
=> end_scene
-- partners
halloran laughing: Partners! Two tinkerers and a cat. The dock won't know what's hit it.
! set shop_partners
=> end_scene
-- mine
halloran happy: Good. That's exactly right. I'll hold the ladder while you paint.
! set shop_mine
-- end_scene
* Sprocket climbs into Halloran's lap and goes to sleep, and for a while neither of you says anything at all.
end
