# Starship Tinkerer: Design

A cozy starship repair shop on a small orbital dock. Friendly pilots bring in their ships with a
need, a budget and a story. You diagnose, repair, modify and paint each ship to suit its owner, and
get paid in pebs, spare parts or snacks. You can also buy junk, fix it up and flip it, whole ships
included. Between jobs you take tea breaks and read on a translucent tablet. After hours you rebuild
your own battered ship, and if you feel like it, you race it.

Inspirations: Dressmaker for the commission loop and the cast of regulars with their own arcs, Becky
Chambers' Monk and Robot books for the gentle solarpunk mood (and the pebs), Mario Kart for how
racing feels, and Gran Turismo and Need for Speed for how much upgrades matter.

## Pillars

1. **Craft feels good in your hands.** Every repair is a small tactile act: tightening, soldering,
   aligning, painting. Doing it well is satisfying, and doing it badly is still fine.
2. **People come first.** Each job belongs to someone. What they ask for reveals who they are, and
   the ship you hand back changes what happens to them next.
3. **Upgrades really matter.** A part changes how a ship drives in ways you can feel on the track
   and see in the numbers.
4. **Nothing is urgent.** There's no rent, no failure and no timer you can lose to. Racing is
   optional, and a player who only ever works on ships can finish every story.
5. **Cozy with a wink.** The tone is warm and kind, and the writing and mischief have enough bite to
   keep adults entertained. Violence stays at Mario Kart level.

## Setting

**Quietwater Dock** is a small repair-and-supply ring circling Bramble, a banded gas giant at the
edge of a settled system. Haulers, pilgrims, retirees and hobby racers stop here. The station grows
its own food in drum gardens, runs on sunlight and patience, and settles most debts by trade. Pebs
are the common currency, though half the dock would rather pay in jam.

You've just inherited a cluttered hangar shop from an old tinkerer named Halloran, who retired to a
comet-watching outpost. Halloran writes you letters on the tablet now and then, and the shop is full
of their half-finished projects, including the wreck you'll rebuild as your own ship.

**You** are never shown on screen, as in Dressmaker. At the start you choose a name and pronouns
(she/her, he/him, they/them, or custom), and pilots, letters and gossip use them. The story is told
through the pilots, the ships and the work on your bench.

## The day

A day is a sequence of moments you move through at your own pace. The clock only advances when you
choose an activity.

| Moment | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Morning | Open the shop. The tablet has mail, the station bulletin and new commission requests. |
| Commissions | A pilot arrives, tells you what they need, and agrees on a budget or barter. You can take up to two jobs a day. |
| Workbench | Diagnose, repair, modify, tune and paint. This is the core of the game. |
| Handoff | The pilot looks over the ship, takes it for a spin, and reacts. Payment, gifts and story beats follow. |
| Tea break | Any time between jobs. Pick a tea and a cake, read something, and get a gentle buff for the next task. |
| Evening | Work on your own ship, shop the parts market, or head to the track if a race is on. |
| Night | Sleep. The day's skill XP is added up and a short scene may play. |

## Commissions

Modeled on Dressmaker's commissions. A pilot comes in with three things.

- **A need** tied to something in their life: "I'm taking my grandkids to see the comet bloom and the
  cabin rattles so loud they can't hear me."
- **A taste** you learn over repeat visits: some like it shiny, some like it quiet, some want every
  dial labeled.
- **A budget**, in pebs, barter or both.

The job board shows each request in the pilot's own words. Reading between the lines is part of the
skill. "Can't hear me" points to a loose panel or a worn dampener, plus a chance to install a quieter
drive the pilot didn't think to ask for.

### Grading the result

Each finished job scores on three axes, shown after the handoff as the pilot's reaction:

- **Fit**: did the ship meet the need and suit their taste?
- **Quality**: how well were the repairs and mods done (skill level plus how the minigames went)?
- **Value**: did you stay within budget, or give them a gift by going over?

Good results raise your **rapport** with that pilot, which unlocks the next step in their story.
Weak results never lock you out. The pilot comes back with a smaller follow-up job and the story
waits.

### Mischief

When a pilot is rude, haggles you down or is unkind to someone, you can get even. Sabotage is real
and has real consequences, but its damage stays at Mario Kart level, cartoonish and survivable.

- **Pranks** at the gentle end: a horn that honks like a goose, upholstery in eye-watering plaid, a
  dashboard bobblehead of the pilot's rival.
- **Sabotage** at the sharper end: a drive that coughs smoke rings at takeoff, a cockpit that
  slowly fills with packing foam, a race ship that spins out on the first hairpin while the stands
  cheer.

Mischief costs rapport and some reputation around the dock, and hawkers and pilots gossip about it.
It also opens story branches of its own. Some pilots take the hint and soften, some escalate into a
long-running prank war, and a few turn out to have deserved it more than you knew. Nobody gets hurt,
and every ship can be fixed (by you, for a fee, with a knowing look).

## Workbench

The ship sits on a slowly turning cradle in 3D. You orbit the camera, click a section, and work on
it.

1. **Diagnose.** Sweep a scanner over the hull. Faults show up as warm spots, rattles and flickers.
   A higher Diagnostics skill shows faults sooner and lists their cause.
2. **Repair.** Each fault type has a short tactile minigame:
   - Seam welding: trace a bead along a seam at a steady speed.
   - Rewiring: match colored leads to terminals before the capacitor drains (it refills gently if you
     run out).
   - Calibration: turn two dials until the waveforms line up.
   - Dent pulling: tap around a dent from the outside in.
3. **Modify.** Swap parts into the ship's sockets (engine, thrusters, fins, cockpit, cabin, cargo,
   lights, horn). Parts come from your inventory, the market or barter.
4. **Tune.** Sliders with real trade-offs: gear ratio (acceleration against top speed), fin angle
   (grip against drag), drive damping (comfort against response).
5. **Paint and dress.** Paint zones, decals, cabin upholstery, dashboard trinkets. This is how you
   match a pilot's taste.

Teas and skills make minigames gentler without removing them. A fully skilled tinkerer still does
the welds, but the bead forgives more.

### Repairs you can see

The work happens on the ship itself, so a finished job looks finished.

- **Faults show on the hull once found.** A cracked seam glows hot along a jagged line, frayed wires
  spill out of an open hatch and spit sparks, a miscalibrated light stutters, a dent is a dark
  crumple. Before the scan they only give themselves away now and then, with a wisp of smoke or a
  rattle.
- **The camera moves in.** Starting a repair swings the view to the spot and leans close, so the
  minigame feels like working on that panel. When it ends, the fix plays out on the ship: a shower of
  sparks and a cooling bead, wires drawn back in and a hatch snapping shut, a dent popping out with a
  clunk. Then the view eases back.
- **Your work leaves marks.** Fresh weld beads and new hatch plates stay on the hull, so the handoff
  turntable shows exactly what you did.
- **Parts arrive physically.** A swapped part drops onto its socket with a bounce and a puff, and the
  old one lifts away.
- **Paint sweeps on** in a mist from nose to tail.
- **Grime and wear.** Ships come in dusty and scuffed. Scrubbing them down by dragging over the hull
  is its own small, satisfying job, and a clean ship fits most pilots' taste a little better.

## Payment and barter

- **Pebs** buy parts, tools, teas, cakes and books.
- **Barter** comes from pilots who'd rather trade: a crate of salvaged thrusters, a jar of moon-plum
  jam, a rare tea, a paperback for the tablet. Some barter items can't be bought anywhere else, which
  makes barter jobs worth taking even when they pay less.
- **Tips and gifts** follow great results and story milestones.

There are no bills, so pebs only ever buy you things you want.

## Trading and flipping

Anything you own can be bought, sold or swapped: parts, whole ships, gadgets, trinkets, teas and
oddities.

- **Hawkers** drop by the shop now and then with a cart of wares: a box of mystery fittings, a
  cracked but antique cockpit dome, a crate of "probably working" thrusters. Some of it is junk,
  some is treasure, and Diagnostics helps you tell which. Hawkers are characters too, with running
  jokes and a few small arcs of their own.
- **Upcycling.** Restore, rebuild or combine what you buy and sell it on. A rusted fin restored and
  repainted is worth far more than you paid. Some combinations make unique parts.
- **Junker ships.** The dock's salvage yard and the occasional desperate seller offer whole ships
  that barely fly. Buy one, trick it out, then sell it to a pilot looking for exactly that kind of
  ship, keep it as a second race ship, or strip it for parts. Flipped ships go to buyers with their
  own tastes, the way commissions do, so a well-matched build sells for more.
- **The market** sets prices that drift with what's in demand around the dock. A race series on
  Bramble's rings pushes up the price of grippy fins, and the bulletin hints at what's coming.
- **Selling** goes through the market board, through hawkers who'll buy as well as sell, or straight
  to pilots whose wish lists you've learned.

Flipping is another way to play. A player can earn mostly from commissions, mostly from flipping,
or mix the two.

## Easter eggs and secrets

The dock rewards poking around.

- Ships that come in for work sometimes hide things: a note tucked behind a panel, an old pilot's
  lucky charm, a stowaway space-moth, a part far rarer than the ship it's in. Finds can be kept,
  returned to the owner (who may have a story about it), or sold.
- Halloran's hangar is full of half-finished projects, locked drawers and labeled boxes that open as
  your skills and knowledge grow.
- Some gadgets and parts are secrets themselves: a horn that plays a tune from the serialized novel,
  a paint finish that only appears in Bramble's shadow, a thruster that sneezes.
- Callbacks reward attention, like a trinket from one pilot's story turning up in another's ship.

## Evergreen play

The main cast's arcs are written by hand and they end. The shop doesn't. Each playthrough draws on a
seed, and the days after (and between) the big arcs are filled by procedurally cast stories. The
rule is **authored stories, procedural casting**. Every line of prose is written by a person.
Generation decides who walks in, what they fly, what's wrong with it, which small story they're
part of and how it turns out.

- **The run seed** decides which side characters turn up and when, the market's starting prices and
  how they drift, which hawkers visit, what's in the salvage yard, where easter eggs are hidden, and
  the race calendar's rule twists. Two playthroughs share the cast and the big arcs but rarely a
  week.
- **Walk-in customers** are generated aliens. A species sets their silhouette (head shape, one to
  three eyes, antennae, frills, crests, gills) and a naming style. Each walk-in also gets a palette,
  two personality traits (fussy, generous, chatty, haggler, nervous, show-off, sentimental,
  secretive...), a want (quiet, speed, comfort, looks, cargo, grip), a budget and a way of paying.
  Their ship is a kit hull with its own parts, wear and faults, and their wants become a checklist
  the same way the cast's do.
- **Storylets** are small authored arcs of two to five beats with open roles. Each beat has
  conditions (day, reputation, flags, a trait on the cast member) and its dialogue is written with
  slots and trait variants, so a haggler and a sentimental customer play the same story
  differently. Some examples:
  - *The heirloom*: a scan finds something tucked behind a panel. Return it and its owner comes back
    with the story behind it, and maybe a gift. Sell it and someone else turns up looking for it.
  - *The rival shop*: a walk-in compares your quote with a slick shop across the ring, and a later
    beat shows who was right.
  - *Courting*: a nervous pilot wants their ship to impress someone. The date's verdict arrives as a
    letter.
  - *The stowaway*: a space-moth is nesting in a cabin vent, and the choice of what to do with it
    echoes for days.
  - *The bet*: two regulars bet on whose ship you can make faster, and both bring them in.
  - *Lost in transit*: a courier's cargo bay hides a parcel addressed to someone on the dock.
- **Regulars.** Walk-ins you do good work for come back. They keep their name, face and ship, and
  remember how you treated them, so a generated customer can grow into a regular with a storylet arc
  of their own. Mischief is remembered too.
- **After the arcs.** The main cast don't vanish when their stories end. They send letters, drop in
  for tea, enter races and appear in storylets as themselves.
- **Things that keep turning over**: the commission board, a seasonal race calendar with rotating cup
  rules, market events (a fin shortage, a paint fad), hawkers, junkers to flip, and collections to
  fill (a codex of species you've met, trinkets, paint swatches, the serialized novel). Shop
  reputation tiers bring bigger and stranger ships.
- **Faces for everyone.** Generated customers get portraits in the same comic ink, assembled at
  runtime from a paper-doll library rendered in Blender. Each head, appendage, body and outfit layer
  is rendered in white with its ink, halftone and hatching, and tinted in the game by multiplying in
  the character's palette, so shading and linework survive any color. Eyes, brows and mouths are
  their own layers for each expression, so every generated alien has the full set of seven.

## Special events and places

Like the festivals in Harvest Moon, some days aren't shop days. The calendar marks them ahead (on
the bulletin, in letters, in gossip), and on the day the game leaves the hangar for a scene of its
own somewhere the tablet has been talking about. Big story beats unfold there, with the cast
gathered in one place.

- **The Comet Bloom.** The comet Odessa keeps mentioning. The whole dock flies out to watch it
  bloom, and the ships you've worked on are all there, parked in a ring. Odessa's arc lands here,
  with her grandkids, in the ship you made quiet enough to hear them.
- **The Lighthouse of Lumen Reach.** The setting of the serialized novel on the tablet turns out to
  be a real place at the edge of the rings. A pilgrimage there could close the novel's story and
  open a secret or two of Halloran's.
- **Race days** on the seasonal calendar: the Slow Sunday Cup, the Bramble Grand Prix, a night race
  through the ring shadow.
- **Dock festivals**: a lantern night, the drum gardens' harvest, a swap meet where hawkers gather.

Events are data like everything else: a date (or condition), a scene to load, who attends, and the
dialogue that plays. Playthroughs vary which minor events happen in which season, while the story
events stay fixed.

## Sprocket the shop cat

Halloran's cat came with the hangar and will not be leaving: a moody tuxedo with green eyes, a
green collar and a gold tag. Each morning Sprocket's mood decides the day. On a sweet day Sprocket
sniffs out a hidden fault, brings you a found part, or curls up in your lap at tea for an extra
spark. On a grumpy day a part goes missing (it turns up on the shelf tomorrow), Sprocket lies across
the workbench and makes repairs fiddlier, or a tin of tea ends up on the floor. Plenty of days
Sprocket just naps. Petting (when offered) and sharing a cake build affection, and a loved cat helps
more and meddles less.

## Tea breaks and the tablet

The tea nook is a sunny corner of the hangar with a window onto Bramble's bands.

- **Teas** each give a small buff for the next task: Steady Leaf (calmer welding hand), Bright
  Ember (faster diagnosis), Drift Bloom (better tuning feel). New teas come from barter and the
  market.
- **Cakes** are collectible and cute. Each one restores a little **spark**, the energy that limits
  how many jobs you can take in a day. Running low on spark just means it's time for a break.
- **The tablet** is a translucent pane you hold up to the window. It holds:
  - **Leisure reading**: a serialized novel delivered a chapter at a time, poems, recipes, the
    station bulletin's gossip column.
  - **Letters** from Halloran and from pilots you've helped, which carry their stories forward
    between visits.
  - **Manuals**: technical books that teach you things. Reading one unlocks a blueprint, a tuning
    insight or a skill perk. Manuals are the main way knowledge grows, beside XP.

Reading is never required, but manuals and letters reward it.

## Your progression

Five skills level up from use, RPG style:

| Skill | Grows from | Improves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanics | Welding, dent pulling, part swaps | Repair minigame tolerance, install speed |
| Electronics | Rewiring, calibration | Electrical minigames, unlocks electrical mods |
| Diagnostics | Scanning | Faults revealed sooner, causes explained |
| Craft | Painting, upholstery, trinkets | More paint finishes and decals, better Fit with picky pilots |
| Piloting | Test flights and races | Handling assists, better test-flight feedback on your own tunes |

Each skill level offers a choice of two perks. **Knowledge** (blueprints and insights from manuals,
pilots and Halloran's notes) unlocks new parts to fabricate and new tuning options. Tools also
upgrade (a better scanner, a finer torch), bought with pebs or traded for.

## Your ship

Halloran's wreck, the *Kettle*, sits under a tarp in the back bay. It's slow, loud and endearing.

Ship stats, shared by every ship in the game:

| Stat | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Thrust | Acceleration |
| Top speed | Speed ceiling |
| Handling | Turn rate and responsiveness |
| Grip | Stability in corners and drafts |
| Boost | Capacity and recharge |
| Mass | Affects all of the above |
| Comfort | Matters to clients, ignored by racers |

Parts come in tiers and families with distinct personalities, so two ships with the same total rating
can feel very different. Tuning sliders shape the ship further. Racing classes cap a ship's
performance rating (like Gran Turismo's PP), so the question is always how to build the best ship
within the class, which keeps upgrades meaningful at every stage.

## Racing (optional)

Arcade handling at Mario Kart's level of accessibility, on 3D tracks wrapped around the station,
through Bramble's rings and down the dock's service tunnels.

- **Feel**: forgiving steering, drift-to-boost, slipstreaming, with the build deciding the ceiling.
- **Gadgets come from your build.** There are no pickups on the track. You fit gadgets as ship parts
  (a bubble shield emitter, a slipstream ribbon launcher, a confetti cannon that wobbles whoever's
  behind you), and each one costs a socket and mass like any other part. Gadgets recharge over a lap,
  so choosing and tuning them is part of building a race ship. Rivals bring the gadgets you fitted
  for them, which can come back to haunt you.
- **Rivals** are the cast. The pilots you work for race in their own ships, which you may have
  tuned yourself. Beating a ship you built is a recurring delight.
- **Events**: a weekly club race, plus cups that unlock with your reputation. Prizes are pebs, rare
  parts and occasionally a trophy for the shop shelf.
- **No penalty for skipping.** Every story arc can be finished in the shop. Races add extra scenes
  and rewards for players who enjoy them.

## The cast

Each pilot has a 5 to 7 step arc that moves forward through commissions, letters and (optionally)
races. These are starting sketches. Everyone on Quietwater is an alien (or a robot), each species
with a strong, simple silhouette and a quirk you can read from across the hangar.

**Odessa Vell** (she/her). A three-eyed, coral-skinned retired long-haul courier with pom-tipped
antennae and round glasses. The third eye only opens when something surprises her. She flies the *Pocket Moth*, a sturdy little
hauler. She wants it made comfortable so she can visit her grandchildren, who are scattered across
three moons. Her arc is about deciding whether to keep roaming or put down roots near one of them.
Pays in pebs and seed cakes. Doesn't race, until a late beat where she enters the Slow Sunday Cup
for fun.

**Brindle** (it/its). A maintenance robot that left its station crew to go racing. It's earnest, literal and
times everything: hellos, sandwiches, rainbows. Pays in salvaged parts, which are often surprisingly
good. Its arc follows it from wanting to win to finding it loves telling everyone about races more
than running them, ending as the club race's commentator.

**Captain Ilse Marrow** (she/her). A sky-blue racer with an axolotl's wide grin, feathery gill
fronds and goggles pushed up on her forehead. The dock's reigning racing champion, who flies the *Heron*. Gruff,
generous and secretly worried about getting slower. She brings you her race ship for exacting tune-ups
and pays very well. Her arc moves from rivalry toward mentorship, and possibly retirement.

**Thaddeus Crake** (he/him). A one-eyed, gourd-headed yacht owner with a gold crest, a monocle and
a curled tentacle mustache, who flies the plum-and-brass *Magnificence*. He haggles, he sneers, and
he is the obvious target for mischief. His arc is about whether anyone has ever been kind to him
without wanting something.

**Tam and Juniper Okafor** (he/him, she/her). Siblings who run a noodle cart out of a converted
tug, the *Brothy*. They need kitchen fittings, cargo cooling and a bigger serving hatch. Pays mostly in
snacks. Their arc is a gentle disagreement about whether to expand into a real restaurant or stay
small and keep flying.

**Pip Arkwright** (they/them). A nervous new pilot with a fresh license, a tiny budget and a
secondhand racer called the *Dandelion*. Their arc is about confidence, from their first terrified
test flight to their first podium in the novice cup, if you help them get there.

**Dr. Sol Yaneva** (he/him). A traveling botanist whose ship, the *Glasshouse*, is half greenhouse.
He needs humidity control, grow lights and a gentler drive for his seedlings. Pays in teas he grows
himself and books for your tablet. His arc is a search for a plant species thought to be extinct.

**Halloran** (they/them). Your predecessor, present only through letters, notes in the margins of
the manuals, and the projects they left behind. Their letters frame the larger story of why they
really left.

## Art direction

- **Ships** are real-time 3D, built in Godot from a Blender parts kit, as in Graffiti Pets. Hulls
  carry named sockets (`engine`, `fin_l`, `cockpit`, `cabin`, `lights`...) and parts snap onto them.
  Mesh names end in a material role (`_paint`, `_trim`, `_glass`, `_metal`, `_glow`) so the paint
  shop can recolor any part. Cel shading plus an outline pass, warm and soft.
- **Characters** are 2D portraits rendered from Blender in a comic-book ink style. Bold flat colors
  are inked with Freestyle brush lines that swell, taper, wobble and overshoot a little, as if drawn
  by hand. Midtones are halftone dots and the deepest shadows are cross-hatched, both in screen
  space so they stay print-sized. Each character has seven expressions (neutral, happy, laughing,
  thoughtful, surprised, sad, grumpy), shown visual-novel style during conversations. Prop icons use
  the same ink.
- **The shop, the tea nook and the tracks** are simple 3D scenes with the same toon look. Teas,
  cakes and trinkets are small Blender models rendered as icons.
- **The tablet** is a UI panel with a frosted, faintly tinted glass shader over the scene behind it.
- **Palette**: warm hangar light (amber, brass, cream) against Bramble's cool bands (teal, dusk
  violet), with soft pastel paints for the ships.

## Music and sound

- **Bundled music** for each place and mood: the shop, the tea nook, the market, race themes and
  story moments. Tracks are written or picked by ttam and ship with the game.
- **The shop radio** is the template's SoundCloud player, presented as a radio on the workbench
  shelf. Switching it on plays ttam's instrumentals and quiets the bundled shop music. Races and
  story scenes always use bundled music. The radio drives the existing `music-feature.js` player through
  `JavaScriptBridge`.
- **Sound effects** carry the tactile pillar: the hiss of the torch, the click of a lead seating,
  the clink of a teacup.

## Technical plan

- Godot 4.5, Compatibility renderer, web export for crap.games, from the crap-games template.
- **Controls aim for parity across desktop and touch, with desktop winning any conflict.** Minigames
  and menus are built on pointer drags and taps, so mouse and touch share one code path. Racing is
  designed for keyboard and gamepad, with on-screen touch controls (the virtual joystick addon Snail
  Sim uses) as a supported second option. Layouts are landscape. When a design only works well on
  one platform, desktop decides.
- Content is data: pilots, jobs, parts, teas, cakes and reading live in JSON under
  `godot_web_game/data/`, so writing a new arc means adding data and dialogue without touching code.
- Dialogue in a small line-based script format with speaker, expression, choices and flags, parsed
  by our own loader. We'll write our own because the needs are small. Lines use tokens for the
  player's name and pronouns (`{name}`, `{they}`, `{them}`, `{their}`, `{theyre}`), including verb
  agreement ("{they} {is|are}"), so the writing never assumes a gender.
- Saves go to `user://save.json` (IndexedDB on web), as in Graffiti Pets.
- Blender pipeline in `blender/`, run headless:
  - `ship_kit.py` builds hulls and parts and exports `assets/ships/kit.glb`.
  - `portraits.py` renders each character's expression sheet to PNG.
  - `props.py` renders tea, cake and trinket icons.
  - Generated assets are committed, so the game builds without Blender.

## Milestones

1. **Scaffold and design.** Done. This document, the template scaffold, and a working web export.
2. **Vertical slice: one day.** Done. Odessa's first commission (diagnose, two repair minigames, one part
   swap, paint), a tea break with one book, one upgrade on the *Kettle*, and one race against
   Brindle and Ilse on one track. Blender kit with one hull and a handful of parts, plus Odessa's
   portrait sheet.
3. **Workbench depth.** Done, with Brindle's and Crake's commissions to exercise it. All repair minigames, tuning, the paint shop, skills and perks.
   Mischief options on the bench.
4. **Repairs you can see.** Done. Faults on the hull, camera moves, fix effects, lasting repair marks,
   multi-step repairs, parts dropping onto sockets, paint sweeps and liveries, grime and the washer
   drone. Alongside it: the alien cast in comic ink, five more hulls, phone support, and the space
   course with pause and touch fixes for racing.
5. **Evergreen foundation.** Done. The run seed, generated walk-ins with paper-doll portraits, the
   job board, regulars, the storylet engine with its first four storylets, and Sprocket the shop cat.
6. **Trading.** Done. Drifting prices and market events, hawkers and their carts, worn parts to
   restore, the salvage yard with junkers to flip or race, trinkets hidden in ships, and Halloran's
   drawers.
7. **Racing.** Done. Three tracks in data (Quietwater Ring, the Ring Shadow night run, the Drum
   Garden twist), each with its own scenery theme; a weekly calendar of cups with rating caps beside
   the nightly club race; four gadgets (ribbon, bubble, tow magnet, foghorn); race any fixed ship.
8. **The cast.** Done. Second commissions and arcs for Odessa, Ilse, Brindle and Crake; new arcs for
   Pip, Tam and Juniper Okafor and Dr. Yaneva, each with two commissions, a choice or a cup, and
   letters; three special events: the Moon-plum Harvest in the drum gardens (day 7), the Close Pass
   (day 11) and the Lighthouse of Lumen Reach (day 15). An illustrated field guide on the tablet.
9. **Polish and launch** on starship-tinkerer.crap.games.
